Why I Built Orbitika: An Offline Toolbox for Old Phones

20 October 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: I built Orbitika because many useful phone apps now need a subscription, a constant connection or a new phone. Orbitika is a free app with a retro music player, location marks, emergency tools, everyday tools and mini-games, designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data and built to run on older Android phones and iPhones.

Somewhere along the way, phones stopped being tools and became services. Apps ask you to log in, to update, to stay connected and, more and more, to pay every month for things that used to be a one-off download. None of it is dramatic on its own, but it adds up, especially if your phone is a few years old.

I’m a solo developer, and I decided to build the app I wanted: one that plays my own music, saves a place so I can find my way back, has emergency tools ready if something goes wrong, and doesn’t ask for an account, a subscription or a signal. That app is Orbitika.

The problem

Phones keep getting more demanding. Apps ask for newer hardware, a constant connection and, more and more, a monthly payment. If you have an older phone, are travelling, or are somewhere with weak signal, a lot of those apps simply stop being useful. And millions of people still use older phones, so that is not a small group being left behind.

What I wanted instead

  • Everything works without Wi-Fi or mobile data. The app is designed to work offline.
  • A music player for the files you already own, with the look of the Winamp era.
  • Tools you can rely on when something goes wrong, including emergency translation in 14 languages.
  • No streaming account and no subscription. The app is free, with a one-time Premium purchase for the emergency tools and extra visualizers.
  • It has to run on older phones, down to Android 8.0.

What’s inside: the five parts

Orbitika has five parts, and each one exists because of something I wanted on my own phone.

  • Retro music player. It plays MP3 and FLAC files stored on your phone, with 13 Winamp-style visualizers and 7 retro colour themes. No streaming account needed.
  • Location marks. Save a place and find your way back to it later, even without a data connection. A GPS fix does not need mobile data, which is why this works offline. I wrote about it in how to remember where you parked.
  • Emergency tools. Tools for when something goes wrong, including a first aid guide, sirens, emergency numbers and a translator in 14 languages. See what’s in the emergency kit.
  • Everyday tools. 24 free tools for common jobs: calculator, calendar with holidays by country, clock, unit converter, date difference, decision maker, discount calculator, stopwatch, timezone converter, reminders, ruler and level, height measure, star map, size chart, system info, sketch pad, score board, noodle timer and more.
  • Mini-games. 18 small games, including Snake, Sudoku, Tic-Tac-Toe and Slide Puzzle, for passing time offline.

Built with older hardware in mind

On Android, Orbitika needs Android 8.0 or newer. On iPhone, it supports iPhone X and newer. I wanted the app to feel at home on phones that are a few years old, not only on the newest flagship, so I keep a small “Old Phone Lab” of older devices (a Redmi Note 5, a Huawei Nova 5T and a Nokia 7.2) and check how things behave on them. You can meet the phones and see my method in the Old Phone Lab post. I am still running my first tests, and I share results on X as I go and write each one up here.

Who it’s for

People with older phones, travellers, students and anyone who wants useful tools that don’t depend on a connection or a subscription. If you’ve ever lost signal at the exact moment you needed an app, it’s for you.

An honest limit

Orbitika is built by one person, so the app and this blog grow one tool at a time. Not every tool has a detailed post yet, and my old-phone testing is still at an early stage. Some features also have limits that I try to state plainly: for example, the emergency tools are a backup and not a replacement for emergency services, and sending your location by SMS still needs a mobile signal.

Frequently asked questions

Does Orbitika need internet?

No. Orbitika is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. The music player plays files that are already on your phone, and the tools and mini-games run offline. A GPS fix does not need mobile data either, so you can still mark a place and find your way back.

Is Orbitika free?

Yes. Orbitika is free to download and use. Orbitika Premium is a one-time purchase of $4.99 (the price may vary by country), not a subscription, so there are no monthly fees. Premium unlocks the Emergency Tools and extra visualizers.

Which phones does Orbitika support?

On Android, Orbitika needs Android 8.0 or newer. On iPhone, it supports iPhone X and newer. It is built with older hardware in mind, and I am testing it on older devices and sharing the results on this blog.

Can I play my own music?

Yes. The music player plays MP3 and FLAC files stored on your phone, so you can use your own collection without a streaming account. It comes with 13 Winamp-style visualizers and 7 retro colour themes.
