Why I built Orbitika: an offline toolbox for old phones

20 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: I built Orbitika because many useful phone apps now need a subscription, a constant connection, or a new phone. Orbitika is a free offline app with a retro music player and 50+ tools and games, built to run on older Android phones and iPhones.

The problem

Phones keep getting more demanding. Apps ask for newer hardware, a constant connection and, more and more, a monthly payment. If you have an older phone, are travelling, or are somewhere with weak signal, a lot of those apps simply stop being useful.

What I wanted instead

  • Everything works without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
  • A music player that plays the files already on your phone, with the look of the Winamp era.
  • A set of tools you can rely on when something goes wrong, including emergency translation in 14 languages.
  • No streaming account and no subscription. The app is free, with a one-time Premium purchase for the emergency tools and extra visualizers.
  • It has to run on older phones, down to Android 8.0.

What is inside

Orbitika has five parts: a retro music player with 13 visualizers and 7 themes, location marks, emergency tools, everyday tools, and 18 mini-games. The facts page lists everything in one place.

Testing on old phones

I keep a small "Old Phone Lab" of older devices: a Redmi Note 5, a Huawei Nova 5T and a Nokia 7.2. I use them to check how the app really behaves on older hardware. Frequently asked questions

Does Orbitika need internet?

No. Orbitika is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data, so the music player and tools are usable when you have no signal.

Is Orbitika free?

Yes, it is free to download. Orbitika Premium is a one-time purchase of $4.99 (price may vary by country) that unlocks Emergency Tools and extra visualizers.

Which phones does it support?

Android 8.0 and up, and iPhone. Check the App Store listing for the minimum iOS version.

Can I play my own music?

Yes. The music player plays MP3 files stored on your phone, with Winamp-style visualizers.
