Offline Emergency Tools on Your Phone: What’s in Orbitika’s Emergency Kit

21 October 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Orbitika’s emergency kit has seven tools: a first aid guide, sirens, SMS location, an SOS flasher, emergency numbers, a survival guide and an emergency translator with phrases in 14 languages. They are designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data and are part of Orbitika Premium.

Think about the last time your phone had no signal. A basement car park, a ferry, a hiking trail, a mountain road. Now imagine something going wrong in that exact spot. The apps you rely on every day are the first things to stop working, because most of them quietly depend on a connection.

That thought is why the emergency kit is the part of Orbitika I care about most. It is a set of tools that are designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data, for the moments when a search page won’t load. Nothing in it replaces emergency services or training, and I’ll say so more than once in this post. What it gives you is a backup: the numbers, the steps, the signals and the phrases, already on the phone in your pocket.

The seven emergency tools

Here is what each one does. Four of them have their own detailed posts, linked below.

  • First Aid: numbered steps for seven emergencies (CPR, bleeding, choking, burns, fracture, drowning and heat stroke), readable in English, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese or Korean. Read the full First Aid Guide post.
  • Sirens: the Emergency Sound tool, with a siren, a tsunami alarm and an emergency alarm, a loop switch and a volume slider. Read the Emergency Sound post.
  • SOS Numbers: the Emergency Numbers tool, with police, ambulance and fire numbers for 50 countries and a copy button. Read the Emergency Numbers post.
  • SMS Location: shares your GPS location in a text message.
  • SOS Flasher: large flashing text that can be seen from a distance.
  • Survival Guide: basics for fire, water, shelter and more.
  • Translator: 18 emergency phrases in 14 languages, for when the person who can help doesn’t share your language. Read the Emergency Translator post.

How the tools fit together

I think of the kit as three jobs: know what to do (First Aid, Survival Guide), get help (SOS Numbers, SMS Location, Translator) and be noticed (Sirens, SOS Flasher). In a real situation you may need more than one: look up the number, send your location, then switch on the siren while you wait.

Why offline matters for emergencies

The situations where you need an emergency kit are often the ones where the signal is weakest: a hike, a long drive, a trip abroad or a storm. Orbitika is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data, so the guides and tools are there when the network is not.

One detail to be clear about: SMS Location still needs a mobile signal to send the text message, but it does not need mobile data. If you have no signal at all, the message can’t leave your phone.

Who it helps

Hikers and campers, drivers on long rural roads, people travelling abroad, parents, and anyone who spends time somewhere with patchy coverage. It’s also for people who live or travel with speakers of other languages, since the First Aid Guide and the Translator both work across languages.

A useful way to prepare before a trip

  • Open each tool once at home so you know where things are.
  • Check the emergency numbers for the places you are visiting. The European Union uses 112, New Zealand uses 111, the United States uses 911 and the United Kingdom uses 999.
  • Pick the translator language you are most likely to need and read through the phrases.
  • Read the first aid topics once while you’re calm, so the order of the steps feels familiar.
  • Keep your phone charged. The sirens use battery quickly.

An honest limit

Orbitika is not a replacement for emergency services or for training. If someone is in danger, call your local emergency number first. The first aid guide gives general guidance and is for educational purposes only; a certified first aid course teaches you far more than any app can. The sirens and flasher help you be noticed, but they do not contact anyone.

Are they free?

The emergency tools are part of Orbitika Premium, a one-time purchase of $4.99 (the price may vary by country), with no subscription. The rest of the app, including the music player and the 24 everyday tools, is free to use.
