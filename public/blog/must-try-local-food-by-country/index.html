Must-Try Food: The Top 10 Local Dishes from 36 Countries, Offline

29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Must-Try Food is a free tool inside Orbitika. Pick one of 36 countries from the dropdown and it shows a numbered list of ten local dishes, such as Hangi and Pavlova for New Zealand or Sushi and Ramen for Japan. The lists are stored in the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

You step off the plane, drop your bag and ask yourself the question every traveller asks: “What should I actually eat here?” You could search for it, but the first page of results is full of ads, and with no signal you can’t search at all. I wanted a short, honest answer that was already on my phone.

So I made Must-Try Food. It is not a restaurant guide or a review site. It is a list of ten local dishes per country, one tap away, so you can walk into any café or market already knowing what to look for.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says MUST-TRY FOOD) and see a country dropdown at the top. Below it a header card shows the country in capitals with the label “Top 10 local dishes”, and under that a numbered list from 1 to 10, one dish per card. It opens on New Zealand, and you change country by choosing from the dropdown.

36 countries, roughly five regions

The list covers 36 countries:

  • Asia: China, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam.
  • Europe: France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Russia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey and the United Kingdom.
  • The Americas: Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Peru and the United States.
  • Oceania: Australia and New Zealand.
  • Africa and the Middle East: Egypt, South Africa and the United Arab Emirates.

What a list looks like

Every country has exactly ten entries, and they’re a mix of mains, snacks, desserts and drinks. For example:

  • New Zealand: Hangi, Pavlova, Lamb Roast, Whitebait Fritters, Meat Pie, Kumara Chips, Hokey Pokey Ice Cream, Fish and Chips, Anzac Biscuits and Flat White Coffee.
  • Japan: Sushi, Ramen, Tempura, Yakitori, Okonomiyaki, Takoyaki, Udon, Miso Soup, Matcha Ice Cream and Wagyu Beef.
  • Peru: Ceviche, Lomo Saltado, Pisco Sour, Anticuchos, Aji de Gallina, Causa, Chicha Morada, Picarones, Rocoto Relleno and Arroz con Pollo.
  • Canada: Poutine, Butter Tarts, Montreal Smoked Meat, Nanaimo Bars, Tourtière, Caesar Cocktail, Bannock, Split Pea Soup, BeaverTails and Bagels.

Some countries share dishes, because food travels. Satay shows up in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, and Laksa and Char Kway Teow appear for both Malaysia and Singapore. That overlap is part of the fun when you’re planning a trip through the region.

Why it’s offline

All the lists are stored inside the app. Must-Try Food is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data, so there’s nothing to load. It’s the kind of tool you open on a plane, on a train, or on a street corner while the roaming bill is still a worry.

Who it helps

Travellers planning a trip or standing in a new city, students on exchange, people hosting visitors who ask “what should I try?”, and anyone cooking a themed dinner who wants authentic ideas to start from. It’s also a small learning tool: spin through a few countries and you’ll pick up the names of dishes you’ve never heard of.

A few tips

  • Screenshot or note three. Pick your three favourites from the list and save them before you lose signal.
  • Use the numbers as a shortlist. Try the first few and see how far down you get during a trip.
  • Compare neighbouring countries. Switching between, say, Malaysia and Singapore shows you what is shared and what is local.
  • Pair it with a decider. If you can’t choose, Takeaway Ideas will pick for you, or use the Decision Maker.

An honest limit

Must-Try Food shows names only. There are no descriptions, photos, recipes or restaurant suggestions, so if you don’t know what a dish is, you’ll need to ask someone or look it up when you have signal. Each country has just one list of ten, which is my selection rather than an official ranking, and a few entries are drinks or desserts rather than dishes. It also doesn’t know about allergies or dietary needs.

Frequently asked questions

How many countries does Must-Try Food cover?

Thirty-six, including New Zealand, Australia, Japan, Italy, Mexico, Peru and Vietnam. It opens on New Zealand.

How many dishes are listed for each country?

Ten. They appear as a numbered list and include mains, snacks, desserts and some drinks.

Does Must-Try Food work offline?

Yes. The lists are stored inside the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Does it include descriptions, photos or restaurants?

No. It shows dish names only, so it works best as a shortlist of things to look for.

Is Must-Try Food free?

Yes. It is one of the free everyday tools. Only the emergency tools are part of Orbitika Premium.
