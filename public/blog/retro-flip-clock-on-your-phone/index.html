Why I built a retro flip clock into Orbitika (and 6 ways to use it)

24 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: I built Orbitika's Clock as a retro flip clock that is easy to read from across a room and light enough to run on an older phone. It has a date line, a 24H/12H switch, optional seconds and a larger landscape layout, and it works without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Why I built it

Orbitika has a retro look from top to bottom, and a flip clock is about as retro as it gets. Old flip clocks on bedside tables and in train stations showed each digit tipping over to reveal the next one. I wanted that feeling on a phone, big and readable from across a room, and light enough to run on an older device.

There is a practical side too. Plenty of people have an old phone in a drawer that still works. A clock is a simple job it can do very well, which fits the idea behind Orbitika: giving older phones a second life.

What it does

  • Flip-style digits. Each digit flips when the time changes, like a mechanical flip clock.
  • A date line underneath, written like "SAT 24 OCT 2026".
  • A 24H/12H switch. In 12-hour mode, an AM or PM label appears.
  • A seconds switch, so you can show seconds or hide them if you find them distracting.
  • A landscape layout. Turn the phone sideways and the clock becomes larger, with the date and switches beside it.
  • It uses your phone's own clock, so it does not need Wi-Fi or mobile data.

6 ways to use it

  1. A bedside clock. Plug in an old phone, set it on your table and use it as a clock you can read in the dark.
  2. A desk clock. Keep it in landscape beside your keyboard, with the seconds on for timing tasks.
  3. A kitchen clock. Check the time with floury hands from across the room.
  4. A travel clock. A simple, reliable clock for hotel rooms and long journeys, with no sign-in.
  5. A classroom or meeting clock. A large, clear time display for presentations and workshops.
  6. A second life for an old phone. A phone that is too slow for modern apps can still be a perfectly good clock.

Tips for using a phone as a clock

  • Keep it plugged in. A screen that stays on uses a lot of battery.
  • Set a longer screen timeout in your phone's display settings if you want the clock to stay visible.
  • Lower the brightness at night so it does not light up the room.
  • Be careful on OLED screens. Leaving the same bright image on screen for many hours can cause image retention over time, so do not leave it on all day, every day.
  • Check the battery of old phones. If a battery looks swollen, stop using the phone and have it replaced safely.

Frequently asked questions

Does Orbitika have a flip clock?

Yes. Orbitika's Clock shows the time in retro flip-style digits, with a date line, a 24H/12H switch and an option to show or hide seconds.

Does the clock need internet?

No. It uses your phone's own clock, so it works without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Can I use an old phone as a clock?

Yes. Plug the phone in, set a longer screen timeout in its display settings and place it where you can see it. Avoid leaving a bright screen on all day on an OLED display.

Is the Orbitika clock free?

Yes. It is one of the free everyday tools. Only the emergency tools are part of Orbitika Premium.
