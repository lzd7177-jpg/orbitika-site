Retro Flip Clock: A Simple Offline Clock with Flipping Digits

24 October 2026 · updated 29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Clock in Orbitika is a free flip clock. Digits flip as the time changes, you can switch between 24-hour and 12-hour time, and you can show or hide the seconds. A date line shows the day, date and year. It is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

There’s a particular sound that I miss: the soft clack of a flip clock changing the minute on a bedside table. Phones tell time perfectly well, but they’ve made it small and easy to ignore, tucked into a corner of the screen. I wanted one big, calm clock you can look at from across the room.

So I built one for Orbitika, with digits that flip the way the old ones did. It does one thing, and it does it clearly.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says CLOCK) and the screen shows the current time in large flip digits, with a date line beneath. Two small buttons let you change the display: 24H/12H and SEC. The time updates every second.

The flip effect

Each digit is its own small card. When the time changes, only the digits that actually change flip, so you see the seconds tick over constantly, the minutes flip once a minute and the hour flip once an hour. It’s a small animation, but it’s what makes it feel like a real flip clock instead of a plain number.

24-hour or 12-hour

The clock opens in 24-hour time. Tap the format button and it switches to 12-hour time, where the display shows an AM or PM marker so there’s no doubt. The button shows which format you’re currently in, either 24H or 12H.

Seconds on or off

Seconds are on by default, which gives you the ticking, flipping look. If you want a quieter screen, tap SEC and they disappear, leaving just hours and minutes. Tap again to bring them back.

The date line

Under the time, a date line shows the day, date, month and year in capitals, for example “THU 29 OCT 2026”. It’s a quick way to check the date without opening another app.

Portrait and landscape

The layout adapts when you turn the phone sideways, so the digits can use the wider screen. That makes it a good fit for a phone propped up on a desk or a stand.

Why it’s offline

The Clock is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It reads the time from your phone, so there’s nothing to download and no account to sign in to. It works in a tent or on a plane as well as at home.

Who it helps

People who want a big readable clock on a desk or bedside table, students with a clock on the desk during study sessions, travellers who want a clear display on a hotel nightstand, and anyone who likes the retro look. It’s also nice for a presentation or a workshop where you want the time visible across the room.

A few tips

  • Prop the phone sideways. Landscape gives the biggest digits.
  • Turn seconds off at night. A still screen is less distracting than one that flips every second.
  • Check your screen timeout. If you want the clock to stay on, set your phone’s own screen timeout to a longer time.
  • Plug in if it’s staying on. A screen left on uses battery.

An honest limit

This is only a clock. It has no alarms, timers or second time zones, and it shows your phone’s own time. If you need another time zone, see how to convert time zones for the maths.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch between 24-hour and 12-hour time?

Yes. The clock opens in 24-hour time, and the 24H/12H button switches formats. In 12-hour time an AM or PM marker is shown.

Can I hide the seconds?

Yes. Tap the SEC button to hide or show them. They are on by default.

Does it show the date?

Yes. A date line under the time shows the day, date, month and year, for example THU 29 OCT 2026.

Does it have an alarm?

No. It is only a clock, with no alarms, timers or second time zones.

Does the Clock work offline?

Yes. It reads the time from your phone and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
