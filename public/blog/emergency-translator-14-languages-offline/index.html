Emergency Translator: 18 Ready-Made Phrases in 14 Languages, Offline

29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Emergency Translator in Orbitika has 18 ready-made emergency phrases in 14 languages, including Japanese, Thai, Arabic and Spanish. Choose a language, and each phrase appears in large text with the English meaning underneath and a copy button. It is part of the Emergency tools in Orbitika Premium and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Imagine you’re in a country where you don’t speak a word of the language. You lose your wallet, or someone in your group feels unwell, and the person in front of you is trying to help but can’t understand you. The translation app needs data. The data needs signal. The signal is nowhere.

That’s the situation the Emergency Translator is for. It doesn’t try to translate anything you type. It does something simpler and more reliable: it gives you the 18 phrases you’re most likely to need in an emergency, already written, in 14 languages, and designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says EMERGENCY TRANSLATOR) and see a “Select language” dropdown and an “Emergency phrases” list with a badge that says 18 phrases. Choose a language and the list updates. Each phrase is shown in large text in that language, with the English meaning in smaller text underneath, so you can read it and show it at the same time. A copy button on the right copies the translated text to your clipboard, and a short “Copied!” message confirms it.

14 languages

English, Malay, Chinese (Traditional), Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, French, German, Spanish, Arabic, Russian and Portuguese. Chinese is the Traditional script.

The 18 phrases

Here they are, grouped by situation (the app lists them in one list):

  • Getting urgent help: “I need help!”, “This is an emergency!”, “Please call the police!”, “Call an ambulance!”, “There is a fire!”
  • Health: “I need a doctor!”, “I need medicine!”, “Where is the hospital?”, “Where is the pharmacy?”
  • Getting around and sorting things out: “Where is the embassy?”, “I need a taxi!”, “Where is the ATM?”
  • Basics: “I need water!”, “I need food!”, “Can I charge my phone?”
  • Losing things and people: “I lost my family!”, “I lost my wallet!”
  • Making contact: “Do you speak English?”

Why it’s offline

Every phrase in every language is stored inside the app, so the Emergency Translator is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. There’s no live translation to wait for, which is exactly what you want when the signal is weak and the situation is stressful.

Who it helps

Travellers, students on exchange, people visiting family abroad, volunteers and anyone who spends time somewhere they don’t speak the language. It also helps the person you’re talking to: seeing a clear phrase in their own language is much easier than guessing at gestures.

A few tips

  • Pick your destination’s language before you travel and read through the list once.
  • Show the screen, don’t read it aloud. If you don’t know the pronunciation, let the other person read the text.
  • Use copy to paste into a message. The phrase can go straight into a text or a chat app.
  • Combine it with the other emergency tools. Look up the number in Emergency Numbers and check the steps in the First Aid Guide.

An honest limit

The translator is a fixed phrasebook. It can’t translate anything you type, and it doesn’t read phrases aloud or show pronunciation guides, so for languages like Thai, Arabic or Hindi you’ll need to show the screen rather than say the words. It’s a help for urgent, simple needs. It isn’t a replacement for a human interpreter in a serious medical or legal situation, and you should always call your local emergency number when someone is in danger.
