How to Make an Old Android Phone Faster: 10 Steps That Actually Help

22 October 2026 · updated 7 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: To make an old Android phone faster: restart it, free up storage (keep 10 to 20% free), remove or disable unused apps, clear the cache of heavy apps, use lighter apps, reduce animations in Developer options, keep apps updated, cut background activity, check the battery, and as a last resort back up and factory reset. Skip “RAM booster” apps.

Nobody’s old phone broke overnight. It just got a little slower every month until opening the camera felt like waiting for a kettle. The good news is that a lot of that slowness isn’t the hardware wearing out. It’s years of piled-up apps, a nearly full storage and background tasks nobody asked for.

I test older phones for Orbitika, so I spend a lot of time on this question. These ten steps are the ones I’d try first, in the order I’d try them. Menu names vary a little between brands (Xiaomi, Huawei, Nokia and others), but they work on most Android phones from the Android 8 to Android 11 era.

The 10 steps

  1. Restart the phone. It clears memory and stuck background tasks. Restarting once a week is a good habit, and it’s the quickest test of whether something was simply stuck.
  2. Free up storage. Open Settings, then Storage. Move photos and videos to a computer or cloud storage. A phone that’s almost full gets slow, because it has little room to work, so aim to keep roughly 10 to 20% free.
  3. Remove or disable apps you don’t use. In Settings, then Apps, uninstall what you don’t need. Pre-installed apps that can’t be removed can often be disabled, which stops them running and updating.
  4. Clear the cache of heavy apps. In Settings, then Apps, choose the app, then Storage, then Clear cache. Don’t tap Clear data unless you want to reset the app, because that deletes its settings and sign-ins.
  5. Use lighter versions of apps. Many big apps have a Lite version, or you can use the website in your browser instead. A lighter app needs less memory and storage.
  6. Reduce animations. Turn on Developer options (tap Build number in About phone seven times), then set Window animation scale, Transition animation scale and Animator duration scale to 0.5x or off. The phone feels quicker even though the hardware is the same.
  7. Update the apps you keep. App updates often include fixes. If you no longer use an app, remove it instead of updating it.
  8. Cut what runs in the background. Remove unused widgets and live wallpapers, and turn on battery saver or data saver for apps that don’t need to be active all the time.
  9. Check the battery. A worn battery can cause slowdowns and sudden shutdowns. If the battery looks swollen or the back is lifting, stop using the phone and have the battery replaced safely.
  10. Back up, then factory reset as a last resort. If the phone is full of years of old apps and data, a reset often makes it feel much cleaner. Back up everything first, because a reset erases it all.

A quick way to find the culprit

If the phone is slow only sometimes, try Safe mode (on most phones, press and hold the Power off option and choose Safe mode). It starts the phone without your downloaded apps. If it’s fast in Safe mode, one of your apps is the problem, and you can remove them one at a time. If it’s still slow, the cause is more likely storage, the battery or the hardware.

What not to bother with

Task killers and “RAM booster” apps usually don’t help. Android manages memory itself, and closing apps over and over makes them restart, which can use more battery. They’re also often full of ads.

Who this helps

Anyone holding on to a phone that’s a few years old, parents with a hand-me-down phone, students on a budget, and people who’d rather fix than replace.

How much will it help?

That’s what the Old Phone Lab is for. I plan to try these steps on a Redmi Note 5, a Huawei Nova 5T and a Nokia 7.2, and publish what changes, with timings. Until then, treat the list as good practice, not a promise.

An honest limit

These steps can’t fix old hardware. If the phone no longer gets security updates, has a failing battery or only has a few gigabytes of storage in total, you’ll reach a point where a replacement battery or a newer phone is the better answer. And the steps vary by brand, so look for the nearest menu name if yours differs.

One more idea: use lighter tools

A lot of slowness comes from heavy apps, not the phone itself. Replacing several single-purpose apps with one small offline app (a calculator, a stopwatch, a unit converter, a music player) frees storage and memory. Frequently asked questions

Why is my old Android phone so slow?

Usually because storage is nearly full, too many apps run in the background, apps have grown heavier over the years, or the battery has worn. Hardware age is only part of the cause.

How much storage should I keep free?

Aim for roughly 10 to 20% free. A nearly full phone slows down.

Do RAM booster apps work?

Usually not. Android manages memory itself, and closing apps repeatedly makes them restart, which can use more battery.

Does reducing animations really help?

It makes the phone feel quicker because transitions are shorter, even though the hardware is the same. You set the animation scales in Developer options.

Should I factory reset my phone?

Only as a last resort, after backing up everything. It often makes a phone with years of old apps feel cleaner.
