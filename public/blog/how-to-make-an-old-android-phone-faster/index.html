How to make an old Android phone faster: 10 steps

22 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: to make an old Android phone faster, restart it, free up storage, remove apps you do not use, clear the cache of heavy apps, reduce animations in Developer options, and replace a worn battery. A factory reset is the last step, after you have backed up.

Menu names vary a little between brands (Xiaomi, Huawei, Nokia and others), but these steps work on most Android phones from the Android 8 to Android 11 era.

The 10 steps

  1. Restart the phone. It clears memory and stuck background tasks. Restarting once a week is a good habit.
  2. Free up storage. Open Settings, then Storage. Move photos and videos to a computer or cloud storage. A phone that is almost full gets slow, so aim to keep roughly 10-20% free.
  3. Remove or disable apps you do not use. In Settings, then Apps, uninstall what you do not need. Pre-installed apps that cannot be removed can often be disabled.
  4. Clear the cache of heavy apps. In Settings, then Apps, choose the app, then Storage, then Clear cache. Do not tap Clear data unless you want to reset the app.
  5. Use lighter versions of apps. Many big apps have a Lite version, or you can use the website in your browser instead.
  6. Reduce animations. Turn on Developer options (tap Build number in About phone seven times), then set Window animation scale, Transition animation scale and Animator duration scale to 0.5x or off. The phone feels quicker even though the hardware is the same.
  7. Update the apps you keep. App updates often include fixes. If you no longer use an app, remove it instead of updating it.
  8. Cut what runs in the background. Remove unused widgets and live wallpapers, and turn on battery saver or data saver for apps that do not need to be active all the time.
  9. Check the battery. A worn battery can cause slowdowns and sudden shutdowns. If the battery looks swollen or the back is lifting, stop using the phone and have the battery replaced safely.
  10. Back up, then factory reset as a last resort. If the phone is full of years of old apps and data, a reset often makes it feel much cleaner. Back up everything first.

What not to bother with

Task killers and "RAM booster" apps usually do not help. Android manages memory itself, and closing apps over and over makes them restart, which can use more battery.

One more idea: use lighter tools

A lot of slowness comes from heavy apps, not the phone itself. Replacing several single-purpose apps with one small offline app (a calculator, a stopwatch, a unit converter, a music player) frees storage and memory. Orbitika is built for that, and its everyday tools are free.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my old Android phone so slow?

The usual causes are a nearly full storage, too many apps running in the background, heavy apps that were designed for newer hardware, and an aging battery. Fixing storage and removing unused apps helps the most.

Does a factory reset speed up an old phone?

Often yes, if the phone is full of years of old apps and data. Back up first, and treat it as a last step after the simpler fixes.

How much free storage should an Android phone have?

There is no exact number, but keeping roughly 10-20% of the storage free is a common rule of thumb. A phone that is almost full tends to slow down.

Should I use a task killer or RAM booster app?

Usually not. Android manages memory itself, and closing apps repeatedly can make them restart and use more battery.
