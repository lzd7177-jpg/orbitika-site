Tic-Tac-Toe on a 4x4 or 5x5 Board: A Two-Player Offline Game

7 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Tic-Tac-Toe in Orbitika is a free two-player game for one phone, played on a 4x4 or 5x5 board. To win, you need a full row, column or diagonal. It tracks X wins, O wins and draws, and highlights the winning squares in green. It is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Everybody knows how to play Tic-Tac-Toe, and everybody knows how it ends: a draw, nine times out of ten. The 3x3 game is solved. Two sensible players will never lose. That’s why I made the board bigger.

On a 4x4 or 5x5 grid, the game changes. You need a full line across the whole board, so there’s room to plan, to set traps and to be outmanoeuvred.

What it does

You open the game (the title bar says TIC-TAC-TOE) and see two board-size buttons, 4x4 and 5x5, a score panel and the board. Two players take turns on the same phone: X goes first, then O. Tap an empty square to place your mark. X is blue and O is red.

How you win

To win you need to fill an entire row, an entire column or an entire diagonal with your mark. On a 4x4 board that means four in a row; on 5x5 it means five. The winning squares light up in green, and the phone gives a strong vibration. If the board fills with no winner, the game is a draw and you get a medium vibration.

The score panel

At the top, a panel shows three counters: X, O and DRAW. They go up after each game, so you can play a best-of-five without writing anything down. A status line beneath the board tells you whose turn it is, or shows “X wins!”, “O wins!” or “Draw!”.

New game and board size

Tap NEW GAME to clear the board, and X goes first again. Switching between 4x4 and 5x5 also starts a fresh board, while the score carries on.

Why it’s offline

The game runs on your phone, so it’s designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. There’s no account and nothing to download.

Who it helps

Friends waiting for food, siblings on a car trip, parents and kids, and anyone who wants a quick game that’s a bit more of a thinker than the classic.

A few tips

  • Take the middle early. Central squares sit on more lines.
  • Block as well as build. A single O in a row kills X’s chance of a full line there.
  • Try 5x5 for longer games. It takes more moves and fewer end in draws.
  • Swap who goes first. X always starts, so take turns being X.

An honest limit

This is a two-player game: there’s no computer opponent, so you need another person. The board sizes are 4x4 and 5x5 only, with no classic 3x3, and a win means a complete line across the whole board, not three in a row. The score is kept while you stay in the game and starts again when you leave it.
