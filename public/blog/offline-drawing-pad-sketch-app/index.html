Drawing Pad: A Simple Offline Sketch Pad in Your Pocket

26 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Orbitika’s Drawing Pad is a simple scratch pad you can open in one tap. It gives you a white canvas, eight colours, a brush size slider, an eraser, undo and clear, and you draw with your finger. It has no account or sign-in and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It does not save pictures, so take a screenshot of anything you want to keep.

Not every tool in Orbitika needs to be serious. There are emergency tools, calculators and converters, but I also wanted a place to just sketch. The Drawing Pad is a blank page and something to draw with, and that is all it tries to be.

Most drawing apps are big. They have layers, brush libraries, subscriptions and tutorials. Sometimes you only want to sketch a floor plan, explain something to a friend, or keep a child busy in a waiting room. For that, a huge app is overkill. I wanted a pad that opens in one tap and is ready to draw on straight away.

What it does

The canvas is a clean white page that fills most of the screen, and you draw with your finger. A single toolbar above it holds everything you need. On a small phone the toolbar scrolls sideways, so nothing gets squeezed. A tap without dragging leaves a dot, which is handy for marking a spot on a map or a plan.

Eight colours

Black, red, blue, green, orange, purple, pink and brown sit in a row of small round swatches. The one you’re using has a ring around it. Each stroke keeps the colour it was drawn in, so you can change colour as often as you like and the rest of the picture stays as it was. Tapping a colour also switches the eraser off, so you’re straight back to drawing.

Brush size

A slider between a small dot and a large dot sets the brush from 1 to 10. Use a thin line for labels and small details, and raise the slider to fill in larger shapes in a few strokes. Changing the size only affects new strokes. What you’ve already drawn keeps its thickness.

The eraser

Tap the eraser icon and it lights up. Your finger now rubs out what you’ve drawn, using a wider brush than any pen setting, so cleaning up a mistake is quick. The eraser works by painting over in the canvas colour, which is white. That has a useful side effect: undo takes the eraser stroke away again and brings back what was underneath, so an accidental rub-out isn’t permanent.

Undo and clear

Undo removes your last stroke. A stroke is one line from the moment your finger touches the screen to the moment it lifts, so you can tap undo several times to step back through the drawing, one line at a time. The red bin icon is Clear. It wipes the whole page straight away, with no “are you sure?” prompt, so use undo for a single slip and save Clear for starting over.

At the bottom of the screen, a small label reads “80s/90s Drawing Pad”. It’s a nod to the simple paint programs of that era, and that simplicity is what I was aiming for.

Things to draw

  • Room layouts. Sketch where the sofa and the bed might go before you move anything heavy.
  • Quick maps. Draw the turn-off, the building and the car park to explain directions to a friend.
  • Pen-and-paper games. Noughts and crosses and hangman work fine on a phone when nobody has paper.
  • Keeping a child busy. Hand over the phone in a waiting room and let them scribble in eight colours.
  • Brainstorming. Boxes and arrows help you think through an idea before you write it down.
  • Explaining something. A quick diagram often beats a paragraph of text.

Why it’s offline

Like everything in Orbitika, the Drawing Pad is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. There’s no account and no sign-in. You open it, you draw, and that’s it. You can sketch in a bus queue, a waiting room or a field with no signal, and it works the same as at home.

Who it helps

It’s for parents who need a quiet activity, students and planners who think best with a quick diagram, and friends who want to settle a game or explain directions. It also suits anyone who misses the feel of old paint programs and wants a pad that opens straight away.

An honest limit

The Drawing Pad is a scratch pad, not an art studio. It doesn’t save or export your picture, so if you want to keep a sketch, take a screenshot before you leave the screen. There’s no redo button, the canvas is always white, and there are no layers, shapes or text tools. I would rather keep it light and fast than pile on features most people wouldn’t use. For anything that needs exact measurements, like a scaled plan, use a proper design tool.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do in the Drawing Pad?

You draw with your finger on a white canvas. You can pick from eight colours, change the brush size with a slider, use the eraser, undo your last stroke or clear the whole page.

Can I save my drawing?

Not inside the app at the moment. The Drawing Pad is a scratch pad, so take a screenshot before you leave the screen if you want to keep a sketch.

Can I change the canvas colour or redo a stroke?

No. The canvas is always white and there is no redo button. There is an undo button that removes your last stroke.

Does the Drawing Pad need an account or internet?

No. It has no sign-in and it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Is the Drawing Pad free?

Yes. It is one of the free everyday tools. Only the emergency tools are part of Orbitika Premium.
