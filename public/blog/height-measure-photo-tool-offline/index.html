Height Measure: Estimate Someone’s Height from a Photo, Offline

29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Height Measure is a free tool inside Orbitika. Take or choose a photo of a person standing next to an object of known height, such as a standard door, mark the top and bottom of each on the image, and the tool estimates their height in centimetres and metres. It is an estimate, and it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

“How tall are you, really?” It’s a question people argue about at family gatherings, in school corridors and in front of the doorframe where the pencil marks are. There’s not always a tape measure nearby, but there is almost always a phone and a door.

That’s the idea behind Height Measure. You photograph someone next to something whose height you know, mark both on the photo, and the tool uses simple proportions to estimate the height. It’s a fun, quick way to get a rough answer, and it’s honest about being an estimate.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says HEIGHT MEASURE). A prompt at the top guides you through three steps, and the screen shows the camera view. You can tap Take Photo to use the back camera, or Gallery to pick a photo you already have.

  1. Take or choose a photo of a person standing next to a reference object.
  2. Mark the person: tap the top of their head and the bottom of their feet on the image.
  3. Mark the reference: tap the top and bottom of the reference object.

As you mark, labelled lines appear on the photo, PERSON and REFERENCE, so you can see what you’ve selected. If you misplace a point, tap Start Over and begin again.

Choose a reference

The tool needs to know the real height of the reference object. You choose from a list:

  • Standard Door (2.0 m), the default.
  • Standard Ceiling (2.4 m).
  • Standard Table (0.75 m).
  • Known person (your height): if you’re photographing two people, enter the height of the one you know, in metres (for example 1.75).
  • Custom: enter any height in metres for an object you’ve measured.

The result

Tap Calculate Height and the tool shows the result in centimetres and metres, such as “175 cm (1.75m)”. Under the hood it’s a simple ratio: the reference’s real height divided by its height in pixels, multiplied by the person’s height in pixels.

Why it’s offline

The photo and the maths all stay on your phone, and there’s nothing to download. Height Measure is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data, so you can use it at a school, a gym or anywhere with a door.

Who it helps

Parents tracking a child’s growth, friends settling a height debate, people filling in a form who don’t have a tape measure, and anyone curious about how tall a thing in a photo is. It’s also a nice demonstration of how proportions work, which makes it good for students.

A few tips

  • Stand the person right next to the reference, at the same distance from the camera, so both are the same scale.
  • Hold the phone level, at about waist height. Tilting the camera up or down distorts the proportions.
  • Stand back a little. Being too close exaggerates perspective.
  • Zoom in mentally on the edges. Tap the very top of the head and the very bottom of the feet, and the top and bottom of the door frame.
  • Always enter the height if you choose Custom or Known person. If the box is left empty, the tool uses 1.7 m as the reference.

An honest limit

This is an estimate, not a medical or official measurement. Accuracy depends on how level you hold the camera, how close you stand, how carefully you tap the points and whether the reference object really is the height the list assumes, since doors and ceilings vary from building to building. For anything that matters, use a proper measuring tape or stadiometer.

Frequently asked questions

How does Height Measure work?

Photograph a person next to an object of known height, mark the top and bottom of each, and the tool calculates the person’s height from the ratio.

What reference objects can I use?

A standard door (2.0 m), a standard ceiling (2.4 m), a standard table (0.75 m), a known person’s height or a custom height you enter in metres.

Can I use a photo from my gallery?

Yes. You can take a new photo with the camera or choose one from your gallery.

How accurate is it?

It is an estimate. Accuracy depends on the camera angle, your distance, how carefully you mark the points and whether the reference is really the height assumed.

Does Height Measure work offline?

Yes. The measurement is done on your phone, and the tool is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
