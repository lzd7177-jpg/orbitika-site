Emergency Sound: A Siren, a Tsunami Alarm and an Emergency Alarm in Your Pocket

29 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Emergency Sound is a tool inside Orbitika with three loud alarm sounds: Siren, Tsunami Alarm and Emergency Alarm. Tap a button to play it, tap it again to stop, switch on Loop to repeat it, and use the slider to set the volume. The sounds are stored inside the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It is part of the Emergency tools in Orbitika Premium.

Some emergencies are about being found, not about making a phone call. You are lost off a track at dusk, you are at the back of a crowd that can’t hear you, or you are somewhere with no signal and the only thing that can help is making enough noise that someone turns their head. In those moments a loud, unmistakable sound does more than a speech.

That’s why I put an alarm tool inside Orbitika. It’s a very simple tool on purpose. Three big buttons, one loop switch, one volume slider. You should be able to understand it in a second, because you may be using it on a bad day.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says EMERGENCY SOUND) and the screen shows three large buttons in the middle. Each one has a coloured icon, a name and a short description:

  • Siren (red): “Alert everyone · Emergency”.
  • Tsunami Alarm (purple): “Mass alert · Danger”.
  • Emergency Alarm (orange): “General emergency · Alert”.

Tap one and the button shows a spinner and “LOADING…” for a moment, then the sound starts and the button lights up with a glow and a red “LIVE” badge. Tap the same button again and the sound stops. If you tap a different button while one is playing, the first sound is stopped and the new one starts, so you never get two alarms on top of each other.

Three sounds, three jobs

I included three so you can choose the one that fits the moment. The names and descriptions on the buttons are my own labels for how I’d use them, not official signals that any authority would recognise.

  • Siren: labelled “Alert everyone · Emergency”, for when you want everyone nearby to look up.
  • Tsunami Alarm: labelled “Mass alert · Danger”, for situations where you need a large group to take notice at once.
  • Emergency Alarm: labelled “General emergency · Alert”, a general-purpose alert for when you simply need to be noticed.

The controls under the buttons

At the bottom of the screen there is a small control panel with three parts.

  • Loop: a switch with a repeat icon. When it’s on, the sound repeats until you stop it. When it’s off, the sound plays through once and then the button returns to normal. You can turn loop on or off while a sound is playing.
  • Stop: a red STOP button that appears only while something is playing, so there is always one obvious way to silence it.
  • Volume slider: a slider from the quiet speaker icon to the loud one, with the percentage shown underneath. It starts at 100% and moves smoothly while a sound is playing.

At the very top of the screen there is a red warning box that says: “Use responsibly. These sounds are LOUD.” I added it deliberately, and I’d repeat it here: do not test these next to your ear, and do not use them to frighten or annoy people.

Why it’s offline

The three sounds are audio files packaged inside the app, so nothing is streamed. Emergency Sound is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. That matters because the places where an alarm is most useful, such as a trail, a beach or a car park at night, are often the places with the weakest signal.

Who it helps

Hikers and campers who want a way to be heard, walkers heading home late, travellers in unfamiliar places, and people running outdoor events or school trips who need a loud signal to gather a group. It’s also useful for anyone who wants an alarm sound for a drill or a safety talk, since you can play a sound on demand and stop it cleanly.

A few tips

  • Test it once at home. Play each sound briefly at low volume so you know where the buttons are and what each one sounds like, then put the slider back where you want it.
  • Switch Loop on if you can’t hold the phone. A looping alarm keeps going while you use your hands for something else. The STOP button is always at the bottom when you’re done.
  • Check your phone’s own volume as well. The slider in the tool sets the volume inside the app, so I’d make sure the phone isn’t on silent before you rely on it.
  • Keep the battery in mind. A phone that plays a loud sound on a loop will use power, so don’t leave it running longer than you need to.

An honest limit

Emergency Sound only plays sounds. It does not call anyone, send your location or tell rescuers where you are, so think of it as a way to be noticed rather than a way to get help. If you can reach emergency services, do that first; the Emergency Numbers tool lists the numbers for 50 countries.

It also depends on your phone’s speaker, which is smaller than a real siren or whistle. A phone speaker will not carry as far as a proper safety whistle, so if you hike regularly I’d carry one as well. If a sound can’t be loaded, the app shows a short message saying it could not play the sound.

Frequently asked questions

What sounds are in Emergency Sound?

Three: a Siren, a Tsunami Alarm and an Emergency Alarm. Each has its own button.

Can the alarm repeat?

Yes. Switch on Loop and the sound repeats until you stop it. With Loop off, it plays through once.

How do I stop the sound?

Tap the same button again, or tap the red STOP button that appears while a sound is playing.

Does Emergency Sound work offline?

Yes. The sounds are stored inside the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Does it contact emergency services?

No. It only plays sounds. If someone is in danger, call your local emergency number first.

Is Emergency Sound free?

No. It is part of the Emergency tools in Orbitika Premium, a one-time purchase with no subscription.
