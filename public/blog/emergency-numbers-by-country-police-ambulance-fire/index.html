Emergency Numbers by Country: Police, Ambulance and Fire for 50 Countries

13 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: In many countries one number reaches everything: 911 in the US, Canada and Mexico, 999 in the UK and Ireland, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand and 112 across the EU. Others use separate numbers for police, ambulance and fire, such as France (17, 15, 18) and Japan (110, 119). The table below lists 50 countries, and Orbitika’s Emergency Numbers tool keeps them on your phone offline.

When something goes wrong abroad, the last thing you want to do is search for the number to call. And the number you know by heart, whether that’s 911, 999 or 111, may connect to nothing at all in the country you’re standing in.

This page lists police, ambulance and fire numbers for 50 countries in one table. I checked the list against a public reference of emergency telephone numbers and corrected the entries that were out of date. The same list is in Orbitika’s Emergency Numbers tool, so you can have it on your phone without a signal.

Three things to know first

  1. 112 is widely useful. It’s the single emergency number across the European Union, and many mobile networks around the world also route 112 to local emergency services, even where it isn’t the official number.
  2. Some countries have one number for everything, others have separate numbers for police, ambulance and fire.
  3. Call first, then read the rest. If someone is in danger, call the local number straight away. Operators may not speak English, so keep your message short and give your location.

What to say when you call

  • Where you are: a street, a landmark or your coordinates. The Location Share tool in Orbitika gives you a message with your GPS position.
  • What happened, in one sentence.
  • How many people need help.
  • Stay on the line until the operator ends the call.

An honest limit

Emergency numbers change, and some countries use different numbers in different regions or on different mobile networks. This table is a guide, not an official source. For your destination, confirm the number with your embassy, your insurer or the country’s government website before you go, and save it on your phone. If someone is in danger, call local emergency services first.

Frequently asked questions

What is the emergency number in the United States?

911 for police, ambulance and fire. Canada and Mexico also use 911.

What is the emergency number in the UK?

999 for police, ambulance and fire. 112 also works.

What is the emergency number in Europe?

112 works across the European Union. Many countries also have separate numbers, such as 17, 15 and 18 in France.

What is the emergency number in Australia and New Zealand?

Australia uses 000 and New Zealand uses 111.
