Plug & Voltage Checker: Will Your Charger Fit? 47 Countries, Offline

2 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Plug & Voltage Checker is a free tool inside Orbitika. Pick the country you are flying from and the one you are flying to, and it shows the destination’s plug types and voltage, then tells you whether you need a plug adapter and whether you need a voltage converter. It covers 47 countries and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

There’s a particular kind of panic that happens in a hotel room at midnight: the phone is at 4%, the charger is in your hand and the wall socket has a completely different shape. Plugs are one of the most annoying parts of travel because you can’t find out you’ve got the wrong one until you’re already there.

I built the Plug & Voltage Checker so you can answer two questions before you pack: will my plug fit, and will my devices cope with the voltage? It’s a quick comparison between two countries, and it works without a signal.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says PLUG & VOLTAGE) and see two dropdowns: Flying From and Flying To. It opens with New Zealand to Japan selected, and you change either one. Below them a black panel shows the result:

  • Destination info: the plug types and voltage used at your destination, such as “Plugs: Type A, B” and “Voltage: 100V” for Japan.
  • Adapter check: either “Plug Adapter REQUIRED” (with a note that local plugs may not fit) or “No Adapter Required” (plug shapes match).
  • Converter check: either “Voltage Converter REQUIRED” (with a reminder to check devices like hair dryers) or “Voltage is compatible”.

A note at the bottom adds that most modern phone chargers, which are rated 100 to 240 V, don’t need a voltage converter, only a plug adapter.

47 countries

The list covers 47 countries across every region: Argentina, Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Cambodia, Canada, Chile, China, Colombia, Denmark, Egypt, Fiji, France, Germany, Greece, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Peru, the Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, Turkey, the UAE, the United Kingdom, the United States and Vietnam.

A few examples

  • New Zealand to Australia: both use Type I at 230 V, so the tool says no adapter and compatible voltage.
  • New Zealand to Japan: Japan uses Types A and B at 100 V, so you need an adapter, and the tool flags the voltage as well.
  • United Kingdom to Ireland: both use Type G at 230 V, so nothing is needed.
  • United States to Canada: both use Types A and B at 120 V.
  • United States to the United Kingdom: Type G at 230 V, so you need an adapter, and anything that isn’t dual-voltage needs a converter.

Why it’s offline

The plug and voltage list is stored inside the app, so the checker is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. That’s handy at the airport, on a plane, or in a hotel room with no wi-fi.

Who it helps

Holidaymakers, business travellers, students going on exchange, and anyone who’s forgotten which of their devices can take 230 V. It’s also useful for people helping a relative who’s about to travel.

A few tips

  • Check before you pack. An adapter is cheap at home and expensive at an airport kiosk.
  • Read the label on each device. If it says “100–240V”, only a plug adapter is needed.
  • Be careful with heat devices. Hair dryers, straighteners and kettles are the usual victims of a voltage mismatch.
  • Use a universal adapter for multi-country trips, then use the checker to see what each stop needs. Add the adapter to your checklist.

An honest limit

The checker is a straightforward comparison, so it can be cautious. It compares the listed plug types exactly, which means two countries with slightly different lists, such as “Type C, F” and “Type C, E”, are marked as needing an adapter even though a Type C plug often fits both. It also treats any difference in voltage as a mismatch, so 220 V against 230 V is flagged as needing a converter although most equipment copes with that gap. For anything expensive, check the device’s label and the local sockets as well.

Frequently asked questions

How many countries does the Plug & Voltage Checker cover?

Forty-seven, from Argentina to Vietnam.

What does it tell me?

The plug types and voltage at your destination, whether you need a plug adapter, and whether you need a voltage converter.

Do I need a voltage converter for my phone charger?

Usually not. Most modern phone chargers accept 100 to 240 V, so only a plug adapter is needed. Check the label on each device.

Why might it say an adapter is needed when a plug fits?

It compares the listed plug types exactly, so lists such as Type C, F and Type C, E are treated as different even though a Type C plug often fits both.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The data is stored inside the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
