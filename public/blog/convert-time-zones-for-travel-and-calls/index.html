Timezone: See the Current Time in 56 Places at Once, Offline

24 October 2026 · updated 1 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: Timezone is a free tool inside Orbitika. Pick a country from the dropdown and it shows the current time and date there, with the UTC offset and a 12H/24H switch, plus a Quick Reference list with the time in every other place. It uses the phone’s built-in clock and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

It’s 11 at night and you’re wondering whether it’s too late to call your sister. It’s lunchtime for you, but you have no idea whether your colleague in another country is asleep. Every time zone question has the same shape: “what time is it there right now?”, and the answer you want is one glance, not a calculation.

That’s what Timezone is for. It doesn’t plan meetings or convert hours. It simply shows you the current time in another place, and in lots of places at once, so you can see whether it’s a good moment to call.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says TIMEZONE) and see a dropdown of places. Each is shown as the country and its city, like “Japan (Tokyo)” or “United Kingdom (London)”. Choose one and the screen shows:

  • the current time there in large digits, updating every second,
  • the date, for example “Sunday, 1 Nov 2026”,
  • the country and city, and
  • the UTC offset, such as UTC+9:00 for Tokyo or UTC+5:30 for New Delhi.

A small 12H/24H button switches the display between 12-hour time with AM/PM and 24-hour time. The tool opens on New Zealand.

The Quick Reference list

Below the main clock is a QUICK REFERENCE section: a list of every place in the tool with its current time next to it. You can scan it to see who’s awake, or tap any row to make it the main clock. It’s the fastest way to answer “what time is it everywhere?” without opening the dropdown 20 times.

56 places

The list covers 56 countries and territories, including Argentina, Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Canada, Chile, China, Colombia, Costa Rica, Croatia, Cuba, Denmark, the Dominican Republic, Egypt, Fiji, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hawaii, Hong Kong, Iceland, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Kenya, Malaysia, Mexico, Morocco, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Peru, the Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Puerto Rico, Russia, Samoa, Singapore, South Africa, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, Tanzania, Thailand, Turkey, the UAE, the United Kingdom, the United States and Vietnam.

Each place uses one city’s time zone. Countries that span several zones show their best-known city: the United States uses New York, Canada uses Toronto, Australia uses Sydney, Brazil uses São Paulo and Russia uses Moscow.

Daylight saving is handled for you

The clock for each place comes from the phone’s time-zone data, which includes daylight saving rules. That means the time shown follows each place’s real clocks as they change during the year. The one thing to know is that the small UTC label comes from a fixed list, so it shows the standard offset and doesn’t move when daylight saving starts. If a place has just changed its clocks, trust the time on the screen over the label.

Why it’s offline

Timezone is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It uses your phone’s own clock and the time-zone data stored with the app, so there’s nothing to download and no account. It works on a plane, in a taxi or in a hotel with a weak connection.

Who it helps

Travellers working out what time it is back home, people with family or friends overseas, remote workers and freelancers with clients abroad, students on exchange, and anyone watching a live event in another time zone.

A few tips

  • Scan the Quick Reference before you call. You’ll see in one list who’s likely to be asleep.
  • Switch to 24H for planning. It avoids AM/PM mix-ups when you’re comparing times.
  • Check the date, not only the time. Across the Pacific it can already be tomorrow.
  • Pair it with the holiday list. Before you schedule anything, check whether it’s a public holiday over there.

An honest limit

Timezone shows the time in another place; it does not work out the difference from your own time or suggest a meeting time, so you do that comparison yourself. Each country shows one city, so for large countries with several zones, use the city that matches where your contact actually lives. And the UTC label shows the standard offset even when a place is on daylight saving time.

Frequently asked questions

How many places does the Timezone tool cover?

Fifty-six countries and territories, from Argentina to Vietnam, including Hawaii, Puerto Rico and Samoa.

What does it show for each place?

The current time, the date, the country and city, and the UTC offset, with a 12H/24H switch.

Does it handle daylight saving?

The time shown follows each place’s real clocks, including daylight saving. The small UTC label shows the standard offset and does not change.

Does it work out the time difference from my time?

No. It shows the time in the other place, and you compare it with your own.

Does Timezone work offline?

Yes. It uses your phone’s clock and stored time-zone data, and is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
