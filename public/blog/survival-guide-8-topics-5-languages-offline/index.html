Survival Guide: Fire, Water, Shelter, Signals and More in 5 Languages, Offline

4 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Survival Guide in Orbitika has eight topics: Fire, Water, Shelter, Signal, Food, Navigation, Danger and Weather. Each gives numbered steps you can read in English, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese or Korean. It is stored inside the app, so it is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It is educational only and is part of the Emergency tools in Orbitika Premium.

The moment you most need survival information is the moment you’re least likely to have a signal. A wrong turn on a trail, a car stuck on a quiet road, a storm that keeps you somewhere longer than planned. A search engine is no help, and a paper book is in the bag you left at home.

I built the Survival Guide so the basics are in your pocket: short, numbered steps for the eight things that matter most, in the language you read best. It’s part of the same kit as the First Aid Guide and uses the same layout, so if you know one, you know the other.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says SURVIVAL GUIDE) and see a language dropdown at the top and a row of eight topic buttons. It opens on Fire in English. Tap a topic and its steps appear below in a numbered list, followed by a disclaimer and the footer line “Stay Calm • Think Clearly • Survive”.

The eight topics

  • Fire: gathering tinder, kindling and fuel; the bow drill, flint and steel and lens methods; and keeping a fire going.
  • Water: finding and collecting water, purifying it (boiling is marked as the best method), building a solar still, and what never to drink.
  • Shelter: shelter basics, a debris hut, a lean-to, a snow shelter and general tips.
  • Signal: visual, audible and ground signals, fire and mirror signals, and sound signals such as three loud blasts.
  • Food: an edibility test, wild foods by season, insects as protein and a list of things never to eat.
  • Navigation: natural navigation, a makeshift compass, the stick-and-shadow method and tips for travelling.
  • Danger: wildlife, water hazards and the mental side of staying calm.
  • Weather: reading the sky, spotting weather signs, storm preparation and surviving cold and hot conditions.

Five languages

The language box offers English, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese and Korean. Changing it switches the screen title, topic buttons, steps, disclaimer and footer, so a companion who reads another language can follow along on your phone.

Why it’s offline

Every topic and translation is stored inside the app, so the Survival Guide is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. There’s nothing to load, and nothing to sign in to.

Who it helps

Hikers, campers, hunters and anglers, drivers on remote roads, sailors and kayakers, and anyone whose plans take them out of signal range. It’s also a useful read at home, for people who like to know the basics before they need them.

A few tips

  • Read the topics before you go. The steps are easier to follow if you’ve seen them while you’re calm.
  • Start with Signal and Water. Being found and staying hydrated come before almost everything else.
  • Pair it with the other tools. The emergency numbers, sirens and the rest of the kit work in the same way.
  • Tell someone your plan. No guide beats someone knowing where you’ve gone.

An honest limit

The Survival Guide is general, educational information, and the app says so in its disclaimer. It isn’t a substitute for training, local knowledge or a map and compass. Some traditional techniques are only approximate or depend on where you are, for example direction clues that differ between the northern and southern hemispheres, and wild food and plant identification can be dangerous without an expert. When you can reach professional help, call for it first.
