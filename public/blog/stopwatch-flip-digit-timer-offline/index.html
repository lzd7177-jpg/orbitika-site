Stopwatch: A Retro Flip-Digit Stopwatch That Needs No Signal

2 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Stopwatch in Orbitika is a free tool with flip-style digits. Tap Start to begin, Pause to stop and Reset to clear. It shows minutes and seconds, and switches to hours, minutes and seconds after the first hour. It is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Sometimes you just need to time something. How long does the pasta need? How fast was that lap? How long did your kid hold their breath underwater? You don’t need a sports watch or a fitness app. You need a big number and two buttons.

That’s the Stopwatch in Orbitika. It matches the retro flip clock, with digits that flip as they change, and it keeps the controls as simple as possible.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says STOPWATCH) and see large digits reading 00:00 and two buttons at the bottom: START and RESET. Tap Start and the digits begin to flip as the seconds count up. While it’s running, the start button becomes PAUSE. Tap it to freeze the time, then tap Start again to continue from where you stopped. Reset clears the display back to zero, and it only becomes available once some time has elapsed.

The flip digits

Each digit is its own card, and only the digits that change flip, in a short animation. The seconds flip constantly, the minutes once a minute. It’s the same style as the Clock, so the two look like a matched pair.

Minutes, seconds and hours

The display starts as minutes and seconds (MM:SS). When the elapsed time passes one hour, it expands to hours, minutes and seconds (HH:MM:SS), so long timings such as a hike or a cooking marathon still fit.

Feel and layout

  • Haptic feedback: a firm vibration on start and pause, and a lighter one on reset, so you can tell it registered without looking.
  • Landscape mode: turn the phone sideways and the digits get bigger, which is handy when it’s propped up across a room.

Why it’s offline

The Stopwatch is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It uses your phone’s own timer, with nothing to download and no account, so it works anywhere you can hold a phone.

Who it helps

Cooks, runners and walkers, coaches and teachers, presenters timing a talk, parents timing a game, and anyone who wants a quick measurement without opening a bigger app.

A few tips

  • Use landscape for distance. The larger digits can be read from across a room or a field.
  • Use Pause for interruptions, then Start to carry on. The time you’ve already counted is kept.
  • Keep the screen on. If you’re timing something long, set your phone’s screen timeout to a longer time and stay on this screen.
  • For a countdown, use a different tool. The Noodle Timer counts down for you.

An honest limit

This is a deliberately simple stopwatch. It shows whole seconds only, with no tenths or hundredths, and it has no lap or split times, so it isn’t suited to timing a race with several finishers or an interval session. It also counts up only; it won’t alert you at a set time.
