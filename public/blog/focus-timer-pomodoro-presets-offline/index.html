Focus Timer: A Retro Pomodoro Timer with 25, 20 and 50 Minute Sessions

2 November 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: The Focus Timer in Orbitika is a free pomodoro-style timer with three presets: 25 minutes of focus and a 5-minute break, 20 and 5, or 50 and 10. A dot-matrix display counts down, it switches between Focus Mode and Break Time, and it counts your completed sessions. It is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Most of us don’t have a focus problem so much as a starting problem. Open a book, check one message, and an hour is gone. The pomodoro idea is beautifully simple: work hard for a short, fixed time, then rest for a short, fixed time. The timer’s only job is to make the stretch feel finite.

I built the Focus Timer to be as plain as possible: pick a length, press start, and don’t touch the phone until it buzzes.

What it does

You open the tool (the title bar says FOCUS TIMER) and see three tabs at the top, a large dot-matrix countdown in the middle and a label that says either FOCUS MODE or BREAK TIME. Below are the start and reset controls.

Three presets

  • 25 MIN: the classic pomodoro, 25 minutes of focus followed by a 5-minute break.
  • 20 MIN: a shorter sprint, 20 minutes of focus and a 5-minute break.
  • 50 MIN: a deep-work block, 50 minutes of focus and a 10-minute break.

The tool remembers the preset you used last, so it opens ready for your usual session. Switching tabs gives a small tap of feedback.

Focus, break, repeat

Press the main button to start the countdown in focus mode, and press it again to pause. When the focus time ends, the phone gives three firm vibrations in a row, the label switches to Break Time and the timer resets to the break length. When the break ends, you get the same alert and it switches back to Focus Mode. It waits for you to press start each time, so a break never rolls into the next session without you choosing it.

Every finished focus session is added to a counter that reads, for example, “2 COMPLETED”, so you can see how much you’ve done.

It keeps accurate time

The countdown is based on the actual finish time rather than counting ticks, so if the screen locks or you switch apps and come back, it catches up to the right time. If you press back while the timer is running, it pauses and closes the screen, rather than leaving a timer running unseen.

Why it’s offline

The Focus Timer is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data. It uses your phone’s own clock, so it works in a library, on a train or in a place with no signal at all.

Who it helps

Students, writers, programmers, anyone working from home and anyone who’s putting off a chore. It’s also good for short exercises and for kids who find a visible countdown easier than “in a bit”.

A few tips

  • Decide the task before you press start. The timer helps most when the goal is specific.
  • Put the phone face down. The vibration will tell you when time is up.
  • Actually take the break. Stand up, drink some water and look at something far away.
  • Start with 25. If it feels too long, try 20, and use 50 when you’re deep in something.

An honest limit

It’s a simple timer. The three presets are fixed, so you can’t set your own lengths, and the alert is a vibration only, with no sound. It’s meant to be used with the app open, so don’t rely on it to alert you after you’ve closed the app.

Frequently asked questions

What presets does the Focus Timer have?

25 minutes of focus with a 5-minute break, 20 and 5, and 50 and 10.

Does it switch between focus and break?

Yes. When a session ends it gives three vibrations and switches between Focus Mode and Break Time. You press start to begin the next one.

Does it remember my preset?

Yes. It opens with the preset you used last.

Can I set my own times?

No. The three presets are fixed.

Does the Focus Timer work offline?

Yes. It is designed to work without Wi-Fi or mobile data.
