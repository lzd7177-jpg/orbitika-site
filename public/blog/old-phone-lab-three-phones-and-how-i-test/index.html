Old Phone Lab: meet the three phones and how I test them

21 October 2026 · by the developer of Orbitika

Short answer: the Old Phone Lab is my small set of older phones (a Redmi Note 5, a Huawei Nova 5T and a Nokia 7.2) that I use to check what older hardware can still do in 2026. Every test uses the same method: a restarted phone, the same timed tasks and a visible stopwatch.

The three phones

Redmi Note 5
Released 2018. Snapdragon 636. Running Android 9.
Huawei Nova 5T
Released 2019. Kirin 980. Running Android 9.
Nokia 7.2
Released 2019. Snapdragon 660. Running Android 11 (near-stock Android).

Why test old phones at all

Millions of people still use phones like these, and most app reviews are done on new flagships. I want to know what an older phone can still do in 2026, what feels slow, and what still works well. I also use the phones to check how Orbitika behaves on older hardware.

How I test

Every test follows the same simple rules so the numbers can be compared:

  1. Restart first, so nothing is cached from earlier.
  2. Same starting point: similar battery level and the same network setup, noted each time.
  3. Timed on camera. A stopwatch is visible in the video, so you can check my numbers yourself.
  4. The same few tasks: cold boot to the home screen, camera to first photo saved, and opening a heavy app to its first loaded screen.
  5. No tricks. I note the Android version and whether anything was changed on the phone.

What these tests cannot tell you

These are one person's measurements on single devices. Your phone may be faster or slower depending on its age, storage, battery and installed apps. Treat the results as a guide, not a benchmark.

Where the results appear

Frequently asked questions

What is the Old Phone Lab?

The Old Phone Lab is a small set of older Android phones (a Redmi Note 5, a Huawei Nova 5T and a Nokia 7.2) that I use to test what older hardware can still do in 2026.

How are the tests done?

Each phone is restarted, started at a similar battery level and timed on camera with a visible stopwatch. I measure cold boot, camera to first photo, and how long a heavy app takes to load.

Do you test Orbitika on these phones too?

Yes. I use the same phones to check how Orbitika behaves on older hardware, and I will share those results as well.

Can I trust the numbers?

They are honest measurements from single devices, so they show what happened on my phones. Your phone may differ.
