Nothing makes you feel as unprepared as holding a perfectly good charger in a hotel room where it won’t fit the wall. The good news is that the problem is easy to solve before you leave, and it comes down to two questions: what shape is the plug, and what voltage does the country use?

This guide lists the plug types and voltage for the 47 countries in Orbitika’s Plug & Voltage Checker, groups the countries that share a plug, and explains when you need an adapter and when you need a converter.

Adapter or converter?

  • A plug adapter only changes the shape of the plug so it fits the socket. It doesn’t change the electricity.
  • A voltage converter changes the voltage. You need one only for devices that can’t handle the local voltage.

Most modern phone, laptop and camera chargers are rated 100–240 V on the label, which means they cope with the mains voltage nearly everywhere and only need an adapter. Hair dryers, straighteners and kettles often aren’t dual-voltage, so check their labels before you plug them in abroad.

Which countries share each plug type?

  • Type A (two flat parallel pins): Cambodia, Canada, China, Colombia, Japan, Mexico, Peru, Philippines, Taiwan, Thailand, United States, Vietnam.
  • Type B (two flat pins plus a round earth pin): Canada, Colombia, Japan, Mexico, Peru, Philippines, Taiwan, Thailand, United States.
  • Type C (two round pins (the Europlug)): Argentina, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Cambodia, Chile, China, Denmark, Egypt, France, Germany, Greece, India, Indonesia, Israel, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Peru, Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Russia, South Africa, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Thailand, Turkey, Vietnam.
  • Type D (three round pins in a triangle): India, South Africa.
  • Type E (two round pins with an earth socket hole): Belgium, France, Poland.
  • Type F (two round pins with earth clips at the sides (Schuko)): Austria, Egypt, Germany, Greece, Indonesia, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Russia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, Vietnam.
  • Type G (three rectangular pins in a triangle): Cambodia, Hong Kong, Ireland, Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, UAE, United Kingdom.
  • Type H (three flat pins in a V shape): Israel.
  • Type I (two angled flat pins, with or without an earth pin): Argentina, Australia, China, Fiji, New Zealand.
  • Type J (three round pins (Swiss)): Switzerland.
  • Type K (two round pins plus an earth pin (Danish)): Denmark.
  • Type L (three round pins in a line (Italian)): Chile, Italy.
  • Type M (three large round pins): India, South Africa.
  • Type N (three pins in a Brazilian pattern): Brazil, South Africa.
  • Type O (three round pins in a triangle (Thai)): Thailand.

If your home country and your destination use the same type and voltage, you don’t need anything extra. For example, New Zealand and Australia both use Type I at about 230 V, and the UK and Ireland both use Type G at 230 V.

Countries on lower voltage

A smaller group of countries uses around 100–127 V: Brazil (127V / 220V), Canada (120V), Colombia (110V), Japan (100V), Mexico (127V), Taiwan (110V), United States (120V). If you’re travelling between one of these and a 220–240 V country, check every device that isn’t dual-voltage.

How to check your own trip

  1. Look at the label on each charger or device. “Input: 100–240V” means it’s dual-voltage.
  2. Pick your home country and your destination in the Plug & Voltage Checker, which compares them and shows whether you need an adapter and a converter.
  3. If you’re visiting several countries, a universal adapter is usually simpler than buying one per country.
  4. Add the adapter to your Travel Checklist.

An honest limit

Plug and voltage details change slowly but they do change, and some countries use several plug types in practice, particularly in older buildings. The table reflects the data in the app, so use it as a guide and check local information for anything important. The app compares plug lists exactly, so it can be cautious: a Type C plug often fits both Type E and Type F sockets, and a small voltage difference such as 220 V against 230 V is rarely a problem for modern chargers.

Frequently asked questions

What plug do I need in Japan?

Japan uses Types A and B at 100 V. If your devices are dual-voltage (100–240 V), you only need an adapter if your plug is a different shape.

What plug do I need in the UK?

The UK uses Type G at 230 V.

What plug do I need in Australia and New Zealand?

Both use Type I at 230 V.

Do I need a voltage converter for my phone charger?

Usually not. Most modern chargers accept 100 to 240 V. Check the label, and be careful with hair dryers and kettles.

How many countries are in the table?

Forty-seven countries, from Argentina to Vietnam.
